Abstract

People captured from drones are extremely small: in the VisDrone-DET validation set, 89% of person boxes cover fewer than 32×32 pixels (median equivalent side 17 px), and drone videos add camera motion blur. This mini-project builds a frame-by-frame person detector (no tracking) tailored for aerial footage on a single free Tesla T4 GPU.

Our pipeline combines five practical ideas: (1) a YOLOv8s detector with an extra high-resolution P2 (stride-4) head, (2) matched 512×512 overlapping tiles upscaled 1.5× to 768 px for both training and testing, (3) a hybrid CIoU + Normalized Wasserstein Distance (NWD) box loss that handles tiny boxes smoothly, (4) box-level copy-paste of small persons plus drone-style degradations (motion blur, defocus, noise, exposure, JPEG), and (5) real video frames from VisDrone-VID during training.

On VisDrone-DET validation (548 images), sliced inference achieves 33.6% AP, 70.6% AP50, and 31.1% AP_small, improving AP_small by +6.6 points (+27% relative) over a 1280 px full-frame pass with the same weights (27.7% AP / 24.5% AP_small). On 288 VisDrone-VID validation frames, it reaches 25.5% AP and 62.4% AP50, while confirming that motion-blurred video frames remain the main challenge (AP50 drops from 67.5% to 53.1%).

1. Introduction

1.1 Motivation

Drones provide a wide, low-cost bird's-eye view of streets, crowds, and open grounds, but detecting people from aerial altitude is much harder than in standard ground-level cameras. Human targets shrink to tiny regions—often 10 to 25 pixels wide. Standard detectors resize an entire high-resolution frame down to 640×640, shrinking these already tiny people until they disappear. Moving and vibrating drones also introduce motion blur and video compression artefacts. As Figure 1 shows, 83–89% of people in VisDrone-DET are COCO-small (area < 32² px, equivalent square side √(wh) < 32 px), with a median side of just 17 px.

Figure 1: Share of COCO-small person boxes (√(wh) < 32 px) and median box side across the VisDrone splits used.

1.2 Applications

  • Search and Rescue: Scanning large disaster zones, forests, or floodplains to spot small human figures quickly.
  • Crowd Monitoring & Public Safety: Estimating crowd density and movement in public events and urban areas without needing personal identities.
  • Safe Drone Navigation: Helping autonomous drones detect nearby people for safe flight and landing.

1.3 Problem Statement

Given a single RGB drone image or video frame, detect all people as axis-aligned bounding boxes with confidence scores. We merge the VisDrone classes pedestrian and people into one class (person) and ignore official VisDrone ignored regions. Each frame is processed independently (no tracking or IDs). Performance is evaluated using official COCO metrics—especially AP_small—on VisDrone-DET and VisDrone-VID validation sets.

1.4 Contributions

  • Train–Test Matched Tiling: Training and testing on the exact same 512×512 tile grid (25% overlap) zoomed 1.5× to 768 px, removing the train–test scale gap common in tiled inference.
  • Small- & Blurred-Person Training Recipe: Combining a YOLOv8s-P2 (stride-4) head, hybrid CIoU+NWD box loss, mask-free box-level copy-paste, drone degradation augmentations, and real VisDrone-VID frames in one T4-friendly pipeline.
  • Memory-Safe Training Patch: Patching Ultralytics' label assigner to process crowded batches image-by-image so P2 training never runs out of GPU memory on a 15 GB T4.
  • Comprehensive Evaluation & Video Demo: Full COCO evaluation (standard maxDets=100, crowded maxDets=500, and blur-stratified splits) plus a demo video pipeline with blur-aware confidence thresholds.

2. Brief Literature Review

Aerial benchmarks like VisDrone [1] highlight why standard object detectors struggle from above: objects are tiny, densely packed, and often blurred. One-stage detectors such as YOLOv8 [2] use Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) [8] to detect objects at strides 8, 16, and 32, but coarse strides lose tiny pedestrians. Drone-focused variants like TPH-YOLOv5 [6] and YOLOv8-P2 [2] add a finer stride-4 (P2) head to preserve small-object details.

At inference time, Slicing Aided Hyper Inference (SAHI) [3] splits high-resolution images into overlapping tiles, detects on each tile, and merges boxes with NMS—boosting small-object AP significantly, especially when paired with sliced fine-tuning. For box regression, standard IoU and CIoU [9] drop sharply when a tiny box shifts by just 1–2 pixels. The Normalized Wasserstein Distance (NWD) [4] solves this by treating boxes as 2D Gaussians, giving smooth gradients regardless of box size. Finally, small-object copy-paste [7] gives the detector more tiny examples during training, while Laplacian variance [11] offers a fast, classical way to estimate image sharpness.

3. Methodology

Every stage of our training and inference pipeline is designed to keep tiny people visible while remaining fast enough to train on a free Google Colab T4 GPU (Figure 2).

3.1 Data Preparation & Matched Tiling

VisDrone categories 1 (pedestrian) and 2 (people) are merged into a single person class. Ignored regions (category 0) are painted flat grey (value 114) during training so unannotated crowds are not treated as background; during evaluation, detections with ≥50% area inside an ignored region are discarded. To avoid near-duplicate video frames, we sample every 8th frame from VisDrone-VID train (3,050 frames) and every 10th frame from VID val (288 frames).

Each training image is cut into native 512×512 tiles with 25% overlap (128 px overlap, stride 384 px) and upscaled 1.5× to 768×768 when fed into the network, turning a 17 px person into ~25 px. Using the exact same tile grid at test time removes train–test scale shift. We sample 16,000 training tiles (90% with people, 10% empty hard negatives) and 1,200 validation tiles for checkpoint selection.

3.2 Box-Level Copy-Paste & Drone Augmentations

Because VisDrone has only bounding boxes (no masks), we implemented an offline box-level copy-paste module: small persons (side ≤ 48 px) are cropped into a 3,000-patch donor pool and pasted onto 30% of training tiles (1–5 patches per tile) using smooth Gaussian edge blending—adding 14,317 extra small-person boxes (out of 94,932 total). Online after Mosaic, we also apply directional motion blur (p = 0.35, line kernels 3–13 px), Gaussian defocus (p = 0.12 if no motion blur), brightness/contrast jitter (p = 0.30), sensor noise (p = 0.15), and JPEG compression (p = 0.20, quality 40–90).

3.3 YOLOv8s-P2, Hybrid CIoU–NWD Loss & Memory Patch

We use YOLOv8s-P2 (160 layers, 10.64M parameters, 36.9 GFLOPs), initialized from COCO-pretrained YOLOv8s weights (219/437 matching tensors transferred). The extra P2 head runs at stride 4 (192×192 feature map at 768 px input). For box regression, we blend CIoU [9] with Normalized Wasserstein Distance (NWD) [4] in network-input pixels:

W22(a, b) = (cxa − cxb)2 + (cya − cyb)2 + [(wa − wb)2 + (ha − hb)2] / 4 (1)
NWD(a, b) = exp(−√(W22 + ε) / C),    Lbox = 1 − (0.5 · CIoU + 0.5 · NWD),    C = 12.8 px (2)

Because P2 adds ~37,000 anchors, crowded tiles caused GPU out-of-memory crashes during label assignment. We patched TaskAlignedAssigner to match images one-by-one whenever batch × max_boxes > 800 (verified bit-exact via unit self-test), keeping VRAM under 5 GB.

3.4 Sliced Inference & Blur-Aware Video Thresholding

At test time, each frame is sliced into the same 512×512 grid (8 tiles for a 1344×756 video frame), run in FP16 batches of 16 at 768 px, and stripped of boxes within 2 px of an interior tile edge. One full-frame pass at 1280 px (keeping boxes with √area ≥ 28 px) captures large foreground persons before global NMS (IoU = 0.6, max 500 boxes). For video output, sharpness is scored via Laplacian variance on a 960 px normalized grayscale frame:

B(I) = Var(∇2 I960),    conf cutoff = 0.20 if B(I) < 554.7 else 0.30 (3)

5. Results

5.1 Training Progress

Validation metrics on the 1,200 validation tiles rose steadily over the 26 completed epochs in the saved log (~11 min/epoch; log stopped mid-epoch 27 of 30, and evaluation uses the saved best.pt). Validation mAP50 climbed from 0.430 (epoch 1) to 0.700 (epoch 26), and mAP50–95 peaked at 0.331 at epoch 23 (Precision 0.790, Recall 0.601).

Figure 3: Validation Precision, Recall, mAP50, and mAP50–95 across epochs 1–26 on 1,200 validation tiles.

5.2 Quantitative Results: Slicing vs. Full-Frame & Video

On DET val (maxDets = 100), sliced inference lifts AP_small by +6.6 points (+27% relative) and AP75 by +7.1 points, while keeping AP_large nearly unchanged (−0.8) via the 1280 px macro pass. Raising maxDets to 500 for crowded scenes boosts DET sliced AP50 to 73.7% and AP_small to 32.5%.

5.3 Effect of Motion Blur (Blurriest 25% vs. Sharper 75%)

On VisDrone-VID (real drone video), motion blur clearly hurts performance: AP50 drops from 67.5% to 53.1% and AP_small falls from 8.7% to 3.1%. On VisDrone-DET still photos, the lowest-variance quarter scores slightly higher (78.1% vs. 72.6% AP50), likely because low Laplacian variance in sharp still images often reflects smooth, uncluttered backgrounds rather than motion blur.

Figure 5: Blur-stratified evaluation (Blurriest 25% vs. Sharper 75% frames, maxDets = 500).

5.4 Qualitative Video Demo & Run Time

We evaluated the detector on a 150-frame clip (6 s at 25 fps, 1344×756) from VisDrone-VID val (demo_out.mp4), producing 5,434 boxes (mean 36.2 persons/frame, range 30–44, mean confidence 0.65, 19.7% COCO-small; 0/150 frames flagged blurry). On a Tesla T4, sliced inference averaged 0.14 s/image (~7.1 fps) on DET val, and end-to-end video processing took 39 s (~0.26 s/frame or 3.8 fps).

5.5 Key Takeaways, Failure Cases & Limitations

  • Slicing works well for tiny people: Matched slicing gives the clearest gain (+6.6 AP_small), though our baseline compares inference modes on the same tile-trained weights rather than a separately trained 1280 px model.
  • Tight localization is hard on tiny boxes: AP50 (70.6%) is much higher than AP75 (26.4%) because a 2-pixel shift on a 17 px box drops IoU below 0.79.
  • Video & motion blur remain weak spots: AP_small drops from 31.1% (DET) to 7.0% (VID) and 3.1% on blurry VID frames due to real motion blur, video compression, and fewer VID training frames.
  • Limitations: All training components were enabled together in one run (seed 0), so individual component ablations are left for future work. Tightly overlapping crowds, frame edges, and frame-to-frame flicker (no tracking) remain visible failure points.

6. Conclusion & Future Work

We built a practical, T4-trainable person detector for drone imagery by combining YOLOv8s-P2, matched 512×512 tiling upscaled 1.5× to 768 px, hybrid CIoU–NWD box loss, box-level copy-paste, drone degradations, and real video frames. On VisDrone-DET val, it reaches 33.6% AP, 70.6% AP50, and 31.1% AP_small (+6.6 AP_small over a 1280 px full-frame pass), and generalizes to crowded aerial video at 62.4% AP50 on VisDrone-VID val.

  • Component Ablations: Train separate baselines (plain YOLOv8s, without P2, without NWD) to isolate each component's exact impact.
  • Temporal Tracking: Pair with a lightweight tracker (e.g., ByteTrack) to smooth flicker and recover people during brief motion blur.
  • Faster Inference: Use density-guided tiling or TensorRT FP16/INT8 export to push speed from ~4 fps toward real-time drone deployment.
